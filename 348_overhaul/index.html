08

Before code

  • ~400 BCThe Greeks imagine Talos, a bronze guardian, and Pandora, a woman "made, not born."
  • ~1495Leonardo da Vinci sketches a mechanical knight.
  • 1770Wolfgang von Kempelen’s Mechanical Turk: a chess “automaton” that was a man in a box. The machine was a lie.
  • 1920Karel Čapek’s Czech play R.U.R. — Rossum’s Universal Robots — gives us the word robot, from robota: forced labor. Factory-made workers. Then they revolt.
09

The field gets a name

1950

Alan Turing

Asks whether machines can think — and proposes a test. The father of modern Computer Science. Some call him the father of AI.

1956

John McCarthy

Dartmouth. Summer of ’56. Ten people, six weeks. He coins “artificial intelligence.” Some say he is the father of AI. That summer hasn’t ended yet.

10

Early years of the AI era

  • first worksProofs, logical deduction, visual analogy. Shakey the robot plans and moves. ELIZA — ChatGPT’s great-great-grandmother — plays psychotherapist. SHRDLU simulates a block-moving arm.
  • and thenMusic and poem writers, joke crackers, even self-driving cars.
  • Boom!Combinatorial explosion. A 5-step proof with 5 choices is 55 = 3,125 paths. A 50-step proof is 550 ≈ 8.9×1034. You do not brute-force that.

The need for search, planning,
and knowledge representation.

11

1943

Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts invent the Perceptron. Frank Rosenblatt implements it in ’58.

That same year, in a press conference organized by the US Navy, Rosenblatt made statements about the perceptron that caused a heated controversy among the fledgling AI community. Based on those statements, The New York Times reported the perceptron to be

“the embryo of an electronic computer that [the Navy] expects will be able to walk, talk, see, write, reproduce itself and be conscious of its existence.”

12

The first AI Winter

  • 1969Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert publish their book, Perceptrons. They say perceptrons are just linear classifiers. They can’t learn a function like XOR.
  • mid-70sDepression settles in. The first AI Winter.
13

An AI Spring

  • early 80sAn AI Spring arrives, rekindling interest in AI, following Japan’s lead. The era of Expert Systems begins.
  • Expert
    Systems    Rule-based. They make simple inferences from knowledge bases of facts. The facts are curated by domain experts.
  • thenSmaller systems turn out to be of little use, larger ones costly to make and maintain.

It starts to feel cold again...

14

The second AI Winter

  • late 80sThe second AI Winter (but work continues by the passionate).
  • the 90sThe second AI Spring arrives. GOFAI (Good Old-fashioned AI), aka Symbolic AI, makes way for neural networks, genetic algorithms, and other machine learning.

AI efforts start to shift focus: from teaching a computer to do things...

...to endowing it with the capacity for learning to do things from observation (aka data).

15

1997

Deep Blue beats Kasparov. A big milestone: a machine beat a human considered very intelligent.

18

Why you are here

To take intelligence seriously as a phenomenon, not a product category.
To look at the human mind without pretending we already understand it.
To build systems that do something useful, on purpose.
To sit with the implications — technical, economic, ethical, moral — before they sit with us.

To keep an open mind. Without one, the rest of this is just vocabulary.
