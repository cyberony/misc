## Key Findings Summary

### 1. Production Trends
- **Overall Decline:** Total honey production has decreased from approximately **220 million pounds in 1998** to **141 million pounds in 2012** - a decline of about 36%.
- **Volatility:** Production has fluctuated year-to-year, with some recovery periods (e.g., 2010 saw an increase to 175M lbs).

### 2. Price Trends
- **Significant Increase:** Average price per pound has more than doubled, rising from **$0.83 in 1998 to $2.37 in 2012**.
- **Price Spike:** The most dramatic price increase occurred between 2001-2002, jumping from $0.91 to $1.37 (a 51% increase in one year).
- **Continued Growth:** Prices have continued to rise steadily since 2002, with the highest prices in the most recent years.

### 3. Yield Trends
- **Declining Efficiency:** Average yield per colony has decreased from **~70 lbs/colony in 1998 to ~55 lbs/colony in 2012**.
- **Lowest Point:** 2009 had the lowest average yield at 53.85 lbs/colony.
- **Recovery:** Yield improved slightly after 2009 but remains below historical levels.

### 4. Colony Numbers
- **Relatively Stable:** Total number of colonies has remained relatively stable, ranging from approximately **2.3 to 2.6 million colonies**.
- **Slight Decline:** There's been a modest decline from the peak of 2.6M+ colonies in the early 2000s to around 2.5M in recent years.

### 5. Geographic Distribution
- **Top Producers:** North Dakota, California, and South Dakota dominate production.
- **Efficiency Leaders:** Hawaii and Louisiana have the highest yields per colony, though they're not among the top total producers.
- **Regional Patterns:** The Great Plains states (ND, SD, MT) and California are consistently top producers.

### 6. Economic Implications
- **Value Growth:** Despite declining production, total production value has increased significantly due to rising prices.
- **2012 Peak Value:** 2012 had the highest total production value at $280.7 million, despite having lower production than earlier years.
- **Price-Volume Relationship:** Higher prices may be compensating for lower production volumes, suggesting supply constraints.

## Observations and Insights

### Production Decline Factors
The data suggests several concerning trends:
1. **Decreasing yield per colony** - Bees are producing less honey per colony
2. **Stable colony numbers** - The number of colonies isn't increasing to compensate
3. **Rising prices** - Likely reflecting supply constraints and increased demand

### Regional Strengths
- **North Dakota** leads in both total production and yield efficiency
- **Hawaii** achieves exceptional yields (98 lbs/colony) despite smaller scale
- **California** maintains high production despite lower yields per colony (likely due to large colony numbers)

### Market Dynamics
The inverse relationship between production volume and price suggests:
- Supply constraints are driving price increases
- Market demand may be growing faster than supply
- Beekeepers may be facing challenges (disease, climate, habitat loss) affecting productivity
